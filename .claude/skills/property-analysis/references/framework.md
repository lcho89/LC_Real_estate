# Eric Chiew's Investment Framework — Rule Set

Source: the user's compiled notes on Eric Chiew's philosophy (Google Doc, Aug 2026).
Benchmarks are stated as of 2026.

---

## 1. The three pre-purchase questions

Every analysis must answer these three before anything else.

1. **Goal / purpose** — own-stay or investment? Own-stay must be in a *performing* area.
   Investment is strictly budget and ROI; sentiment is irrelevant.
2. **Exit strategy** — *who buys this from you in 4–5 years at a healthy profit?* If you cannot
   name a concrete pool of future end-users, the property is a poor investment. This is the
   question that kills most listings.
3. **Opportunity cost** — what is given up by waiting, renting, or holding an underperformer?
   Waiting for the market to "stabilise" costs capital gains *and* borrowing capacity as the
   buyer ages.

## 2. Hard vetoes

Report these regardless of the final score.

| Veto | Rule |
|---|---|
| **2-bed / 1-bath** | Never. Guests walk through the master bedroom to reach the toilet; brutal to resell. Minimum 2-bed 2-bath. |
| **99-year leasehold landed** | Never. Severe lease decay and price stagnation once remaining lease drops under 70 years. Villa Verde: $2.27M in 2022, still $2.25–2.35M in 2026. |
| **Boutique projects** | "Not rich, no niche." Under 100 units is a liquidity trap; under 400 units is avoided for investment. The Lilium (80 units) has had **zero** resale/subsale transactions since Apr 2023. |
| **MRT > 1 km** | Zero tolerance. Target **under 400–500 m**. LakeGarden's 1.3 km walk to Lakeside "will break your legs". |
| **OCR new launch above $2,500 psf** | Below $2,500 psf is "decent"; above is "disgusting" — no room left for appreciation. |
| **Boutique freehold + small units** | "The worst combination ever" (Rezi 24: 110 units, not one unit made over $200k). |
| **Overseas property** | Stay 100% in Singapore. |
| **Sell-and-rebuy at the same value** | "Stupid." A transition must upgrade size (2-bed to 3-bed) or location (OCR to city fringe). |

## 3. Selection criteria

### MRT proximity (non-negotiable)
Under 400–500 m. Direct/integrated connection is the premium tier. This drives both exitability
and capital safety.

### Project size — the volume effect
- **>= 1,000 units** — ideal. Every resale sets a new benchmark the banks then value the whole block against.
- **400–999 units** — acceptable.
- **< 400 units** — avoided. Low transaction velocity means no matching transactions to push valuations up.
- **< 100 units** — veto.

### The MOP pool engine
Look for a pool of HDB flats reaching MOP nearby, especially estates famous for million-dollar
flats (Bishan, Toa Payoh, Dakota, Clementi). These upgraders make 2–2.5x on their BTO and are
the *exact* buyer pool that takes your condo off you. This is the concrete answer to question 2.

### Elite school anchor
Within 1 km of a top primary (Henry Park, CHIJ St. Nicholas, Nanyang, Raffles Girls', Ai Tong,
Kong Hwa, ACS, Rulang). Desperate parents "anyhow buy at anyhow price" — this insulates a
project from downturns. Check whether the whole project or only certain stacks fall inside 1 km.

### Crowds make money
High foot traffic and human density. Avoid "introvert" low-density areas — the "Pang" rule
(anything ending in *panjang*), West Coast, far-flung OCR pockets.

### Segment
- **OCR / RCR** — where the money is made. OCR generates roughly **3x the capital gains** of prestige CCR.
- **CCR** — avoid for capital gains. Low transaction volume, no HDB upgrader pool, foreign buyers
  crushed by 60% ABSD, and a small local pool able to absorb high quantums. Sentosa Cove and
  CBD luxury are the canonical capital destroyers.

### Tenure
Freehold landed is the only landed Eric recommends. For condos, tenure matters less than volume
and location — but freehold *boutique* is a trap, and any leasehold under ~70 years remaining
is in decay.

## 4. Layout rules

- **Three-bedroom minimum for investment.** 1- and 2-bedders suffer investor oversupply, high
  tenancy turnover and stagnant appreciation. (D'Leedon: large 3–4 bedders made ~$100k/yr while
  1–2 bedders *lost* money over 7–10 years.)
- **Minimum 2 bathrooms.** See veto table.
- **Walkable kitchen** — enclosed, with a window, gas-capable ("wok hey"). Induction-only with no
  kitchen window is a kitchen that "cannot walk" — a lower standard of living.
- **Dumbbell layout premium** — bedrooms split by the living area, no long corridors. Higher
  efficiency, higher rent.
- **No pure-concrete / load-bearing interior walls** — buyers cannot reconfigure.
- **Stack matters** — Treasure at Tampines inner pool-facing units carry a ~$100k premium over
  outer-facing. Buying an inner-facing unit at outer-facing price is an "undervalue gem".

## 5. Resale vs new launch ("Old is Gold")

Resale usually wins for pure investment:

- **Immediate rental income** — a city-fringe resale at $6,000/month nets ~$150k over 4 years.
- **No holding cost** — a new launch means ~3 years of waiting, draining $160k–$200k in rent.
- **More space per dollar** — a 4-bed resale for the price of a 3-bed new launch.
- **Track record** — a good resale plus rental clears $450k–$600k over 4 years.

Worked examples:
- **AMO Residence (new launch, 2022, ~$1.9M)** made ~$400–450k by 2025. A **resale 3-bedder at
  $1.82M** the same year sold at $2.325M in 2025 = $500k gains + $80k net rent = **$580k**,
  beating the top new launch by $130k.
- **Lentor Modern** 3-bed at $1.95–2M made ~$370k in 3.5 years, minus $150–180k of rent while
  waiting = under $200k net. A city-fringe resale at the same entry made **$600k** with immediate
  move-in.

### Go direct to the seller's agent
Seller agents often refuse to co-broke, so a buyer's agent may only get you into **1 of 5**
available units. Approaching the seller's agent directly gets you every listing and more
negotiating leverage (they keep 100% of the commission).

## 6. Pricing frameworks

### Entry price is king — the developer 15% profit rule
`raw land cost (psf ppr) + construction + taxes/finance/marketing + 15% developer margin
= fair launch price`

When a developer bought land cheap (downturn, converted business park, en bloc at a low base)
but prices reasonably, that is the undervalued entry.

Land-cost reference points: Treasure at Tampines $676 psf ppr · Rivelle EC $768 · Penrose $732 ·
Tengah Garden $821 · Lentor Gardens $920 · Reserve Residences $989 · Pinery $1,004 · AMO $1,118 ·
LakeGarden $1,260 · River Green $1,325 · Vela Bay $1,388 (record OCR) · Dunearn House $1,410 ·
Cuscaden Reserve $2,377 (2018 peak — negative equity).

### Budget benchmarks (2026)
- **$1.3M** — a 3-bedder is now essentially impossible; you are pushed to 2-bedders or MRT-poor areas.
- **$1.8M–$2M** — the healthy sweet spot: a quality 3- or 4-bed resale near an OCR MRT, or a 2-bed city fringe.
- **$4M–$6M** — pivot out of condos into freehold landed.
- **$4.5M+** — the realistic entry for decent freehold landed, plus renovation capital.

### New Harmony era (strata harmonisation)
From Lentor Mansion onward, air-con ledges are excluded from saleable strata area. This makes psf
look **~$200 psf higher** while absolute quantum is comparable — you stopped paying for useless
space. Always harmonise an older comp upward before comparing psf.

### Sell One Buy Two
Husband and wife each buy under their own name to avoid ABSD. Both assets compound together,
making the eventual move to landed far less painful.

### Buy property to wait
"Do not wait to buy properties, buy properties to wait" — raw land prices consistently rise.
